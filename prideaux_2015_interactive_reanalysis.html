173 lesions from 15 subjects. MALDI-MSI at 30–100 μm resolution. LC/MS-MS quantification.

The Rank Inversion — When Compartment Changes Everything

Why This Matters

The rank inversion is not a quirk of one dataset. It is a geometric property of how these molecules interact with different tissue matrices. MXF is a moderately lipophilic fluoroquinolone that accumulates in cells via macrophage uptake but cannot diffuse through the acellular, avascular caseum matrix. RIF is a large, highly lipophilic rifamycin that binds to caseum macromolecules and slowly accumulates at steady state. The geometry captures both mechanisms through a single number: R.

Section 4

Rifampin Caseum Accumulation — The Steady-State Surprise

Their Figure 3 — RIF in Lesions

After a single dose, RIF signal in granulomas was low across all compartments. But at steady state (after 180 daily doses), RIF accumulated dramatically in necrotic caseum foci.

"RIF accumulated in caseum after multiple doses and remained present at easily detectable levels in the necrotic lesions after falling below the limit of detection in uninvolved lung and plasma."
— Prideaux et al. 2015

Caseum/cellular concentration ratio: >10 at steady state — more than 20-fold higher than after a single dose.

Geometric Interpretation

R_caseum = 3.0 for RIF means the caseum:plasma ratio is 3:1. The drug concentrates in caseum. K_caseum = −0.67 (negative — the barrier helps).

RIF at caseum:
R = 3.0 → K = max(1/3.0 − 1, −1) = −0.667
τ = log₁₀(60/1.0) = 1.778
K_total = 0.1 + (−0.667) = −0.567
→ Concentrating regime (K < 0)

The geometry says: RIF enters the concentrating regime at caseum. The tissue is actively accumulating the drug. This is the negative curvature phenomenon — the barrier has negative impedance, meaning it helps delivery rather than hindering it.

The steady-state accumulation (20-fold increase over single dose) is consistent: each dose deposits more drug into caseum, and the avascular nature of caseum means clearance is slow. The geometry predicts accumulation; the kinetics determine the rate.

Moxifloxacin Caseum Failure — The Drug That Can't Reach

Their Finding
"Moxifloxacin, which is active in vitro against a subpopulation of M. tuberculosis that persists in specific niches under drug pressure and has achieved treatment shortening in mice, does not diffuse well in caseum."
— Prideaux et al. 2015

MXF concentrations in the cellular rim of granulomas were 1.5–2× higher than in surrounding lung. But in caseum, MXF signal was near-zero. The drug reaches cells but not the necrotic core where persisters hide.

This was measured in 2015. The REMoxTB trial (Gillespie 2014, NEJM) had already shown MXF-based regimens failed to shorten TB therapy. The MALDI data explained why.

Section 7

REMoxTB — The $50M Trial the Geometry Could Have Predicted

The Clinical Trial

REMoxTB (Gillespie et al., NEJM 2014) was a Phase III randomized trial testing whether replacing ethambutol or isoniazid with moxifloxacin could shorten TB treatment from 6 months to 4 months. Over 1,900 patients enrolled across multiple countries.

Result: Neither moxifloxacin-containing regimen achieved noninferiority for 4-month treatment. The trial failed. Relapse rates were higher in the MXF arms.

Prideaux 2015 linked this failure to MXF's poor caseum penetration. The MALDI data showed the mechanism: MXF cannot reach the caseous foci where persister bacilli survive.

What the Geometry Says

The standard regimen is INH + RIF + PZA + EMB. REMoxTB replaced EMB with MXF. At the caseum compartment where persisters live:

The caseum compartment is the rate-limiting barrier, and MXF doesn't fix it.
